# Hiring outcomes: remote and sponsorship evidence

Research date: 10 November 2026. Scope: all professions, with India-to-international opportunities as one candidate corridor. Research only; no product changes. This is a bounded evidence review, not a representative worldwide ranking of the easiest jobs.

## Decision supported by the evidence

Do not choose a niche because its jobs are remote or advertise sponsorship. Those describe access conditions, not hiring probability. A better unit of investigation is **existing qualification + occupation + destination/employer corridor + hiring channel**. Separate candidates already qualified for the occupation from people considering years of retraining.

The evidence supports testing qualified trades in specific Australian regions, credential-ready healthcare candidates in specific destinations, and cross-border business/technical occupations. It does not establish that any of these are easy, or that they outperform one another for comparable candidates. Generic remote jobs attract substantial competition. UK international healthcare hiring is a particularly important counterexample to stale shortage narratives.

### 4. Australian trades show measurable recruitment friction

[Jobs and Skills Australia, March-quarter 2026 Occupation Shortage Report, published 3 June 2026](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/download/19956/occupation-shortage-report-march-2026/4025/occupation-shortage-report-march-2026/pdf), Table 6, reports technicians and trades workers at a 55.1% vacancy fill rate with 3.8 suitable applicants per vacancy. Clerical and administrative workers had an 83.8% fill rate and 7.3 suitable applicants per vacancy. The stratified Survey of Employers who have Recently Advertised interviewed about 900 employers monthly; the preceding 12 months covered 11,000+ surveys and 20,000+ vacancies. It covers selected occupations at skill levels 1–4 and lacks guaranteed minimum samples for individual occupations.

Lower fill rates show employer difficulty, not an individual jobseeker's acceptance rate. Skills recognition, work authorization, geography, pay and retention can prevent a shortage from becoming an accessible opportunity.

[JSA's June-quarter update, 16 September 2026](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/occupation-shortage-report-now-available-new-look-employer-activity-insights-interactive-table) puts the national fill rate at 69.1%. The March subgroup comparison above is older and is explicitly not presented as the newest subgroup estimate.

### 5. Persistent shortages identify candidates for deeper research

[JSA, 2026 occupation-shortage findings](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/occupation-shortages-continue-ease-while-new-analysis-reveals-whats-driving-persistent-gaps) reports 279 occupations, or 27%, in national shortage, down from 29% in 2025. Almost half of technicians/trades occupations and 37% of professional occupations were in shortage. Persistent shortages concentrate in construction, engineering, electrotechnology/telecommunications trades, health and early-childhood education. The analysis distinguishes training, suitability and retention gaps; motor mechanics primarily face a training gap, while registered nurses primarily face a suitability gap with training also contributing.

Inference: credential-ready mechanics/tradespeople or health professionals are more defensible research cohorts than an undifferentiated “any remote job” audience. This remains a hypothesis until occupation-specific hiring outcomes and foreign-candidate eligibility are checked.

### 6. UK healthcare sponsorship: actual entry outcomes have contracted

[UK Home Office, Work statistics, year ending June 2026, published 27 August 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work) reports 49,205 main-applicant Worker entry visas and 70,900 dependants. Nursing-professional Health and Care Worker entry visas fell from 22,858 in the year ending December 2023 to 1,125 in the year ending June 2026, a 95% fall.

These are issued visas, stronger evidence of actual international movement than vacancy counts, but not job applications-to-hires or all nursing hires. They exclude candidates joining via other immigration routes and do not imply every healthcare destination behaves similarly. Never combine dependants into a count of people hired. A product thesis based on the old UK care/nursing boom would need substantial revalidation.

### 7. Ireland has a demonstrated international nursing pipeline, with slowing new registrations

[NMBI State of the Register 2025 announcement, 25 November 2025](https://www.nmbi.ie/News/News/NMBI-State-of-the-Register-Reports-86%2C948-Nurses-a) covers 1 June 2024–31 May 2025. It reports 5,136 new registrants and 86,948 people self-reporting current practice, alongside a decline in new internationally educated registrants. [The full regulator report](https://www.nmbi.ie/NMBI/media/NMBI/NMBI-State-of-The-Register-2025.pdf?ext=.pdf), Table 6, records 3,345 non-EU new registrants versus 5,426 in the prior report. India leads the report's displayed country-of-education figures, but adjacent calendar-year and reporting-year presentations differ; do not silently treat them as identical periods.

Registration is not a new job, sponsorship approval or relocation completion. It does demonstrate a credential pathway with real participants. The meaningful niche would be candidates with recognized qualifications and registration readiness, not all aspiring healthcare workers.

## Retrieval limits and remaining work

- Ireland's [official employment-permit release](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/publications/employment-permit-statistics-2026.html) was dated 9 November 2026 when read and links nationality, employer, county and sector spreadsheets through the prior month. Browser extraction could not read XLSX, and a local read-only fetch failed DNS. No unverified 2026 sector totals are used here. Third-party mirrors have different cutoff dates and were deliberately not substituted.
- No evidence reviewed supplies a comparable candidate-level success rate for worldwide remote versus country-only remote versus sponsorship. That question remains unanswered.
- Before a product commitment, choose two or three occupation/country cohorts, inspect live employer requirements, and gather outcomes from both successful and unsuccessful comparable candidates. Track eligible applications, interviews, offers, starts, time to offer, compensation, contract stability, and qualification/visa prerequisites. Do not collect only success stories.
- No source above establishes that a new all-profession job board has demonstrated product-market fit. Hiring feasibility and product demand require separate validation.
