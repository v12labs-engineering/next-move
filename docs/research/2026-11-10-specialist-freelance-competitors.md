# Specialist ecommerce freelance hiring: competitor control

Research date: 10 November 2026. Desk research only; no product changes. Proposed niche: experienced Shopify implementation specialists serving ecommerce businesses through scoped projects and recurring maintenance. WooCommerce is a comparison ecosystem, not a recommendation to launch both simultaneously.

## Storetasker: the closest niche example

**How work is won.** A merchant submits a specific need, a human-led matching process introduces a relevant expert, the expert scopes and quotes directly, and the parties deliver through the platform's payment workflow. It includes Shopify development, design, conversion work and marketing. Candidate screening includes interviews, background checks and experience review. The platform handles escrow and supports repeat retainers. This is a managed specialist marketplace, not an open job-board bidding process. [Official workflow](https://storetasker.kb.help/why-storetasker/)

**Outcome evidence.** Its partner page describes Bedfolk being matched to Satish in three hours and subsequently completing 19 projects together. Cabana Canary's slide-out-cart implementation was quoted at $650, followed by work with four experts. These are named, vendor-published client examples; no dates, all-applicant success denominator, audited revenue or typical freelancer earnings are disclosed. They establish specific repeat-work relationships, not easy entry for all applicants. [Named customer examples](https://www.storetasker.com/partners/mp)

**Who pays.** The expert FAQ says Storetasker charges a percentage of billings between the expert and a referred client; the rate declines with business volume to 10%. The starting rate and complete tier schedule were not published in the reviewed FAQ. The freelancer sets project pricing, so the platform's economic cost is funded through client spending, with a billings-based platform fee on the expert relationship. Do not describe the whole client invoice as Storetasker revenue. [Expert terms summary](https://resources.storetasker.com/become-an-expert)

**Acquisition and geography.** The partner landing page demonstrates an ecosystem referral channel. The expert application requests a portfolio/GitHub, and approved experts claim matched leads. The site advertises payments from 200+ countries; that establishes payment reach, not automatic eligibility of every worker or client jurisdiction. Country eligibility and payout availability need explicit verification before an India-specific promise. [Expert onboarding](https://resources.storetasker.com/become-an-expert)

## Codeable: a comparable WordPress/WooCommerce model

**Process and evidence.** Codeable describes professional screening, a technical task, live interview/coding, an operational exam, a 90-day trial and continued monitoring. Its homepage claims more than 2,000 projects delivered on time and without issues monthly; 97% of experts have at least six years' WordPress experience. These are vendor claims, without a disclosed measurement window or audited dataset. Their practical significance is a highly selected expert pool, not beginner accessibility. [Official platform and screening](https://www.codeable.io/)

Clients and a small number of experts clarify scope before a single estimate; the platform avoids a many-bid price auction. WooCommerce's own marketplace distributes Codeable's service, providing a concrete buyer-acquisition route at the point where merchants need implementation help. [WooCommerce partner listing](https://woocommerce.com/products/codeable-developer-services/)

**Money.** The help center, updated 1 October 2026, confirms a 17.5% client service fee added to the expert estimate and included in the displayed total. Funds are held until completion. On a $1,000 estimate, that disclosed client fee is $175 and the client total is $1,175. This is illustrative arithmetic, not gross margin. Official expert-side deductions were not verified, so total platform take rate remains unknown. [Current fee documentation](https://help.codeable.io/en/articles/894490-does-codeable-charge-any-additional-fees)

## MarketerHire: adjacent, less precise control

MarketerHire's screening page claims 30,000+ successful matches and a 95% trial-to-hire rate, using specialist review and monitoring of early engagements. These are self-reported metrics with unspecified cohorts; the latter is conditional on already reaching a trial. It is not an applicant placement probability. [Vetting](https://marketerhire.com/top-1-percent)

The live pricing page now says custom agreed budgets, month-to-month engagements and a two-week refundable trial. Older official articles and some role pages show $5,000/$10,000/$15,000 plans, but those should not override the live pricing page. Businesses fund the engagement; the talent-payment/platform-margin split was not disclosed in the reviewed pages. [Current pricing](https://marketerhire.com/pricing)

## Implication for our choice

Shopify implementation is a stronger fit-for-us research finalist than broad freelance marketing: specific work samples can demonstrate capability; merchant needs can be scoped; repeat maintenance is measurable; and ecosystem partners can introduce paying demand. That is an inference from the models above, not proof we possess distribution or candidate quality.

Compared with remote customer support, this offers project-level paid outcomes and repeat-client revenue, but introduces scoping, delivery-quality and payment-dispute responsibilities. Compared with licensed nursing, it avoids relying on clinical credential infrastructure, while still requiring specialized technical assessment and merchant acquisition.

Start validation with one service family, such as Shopify theme fixes and integrations. Require evidence of paid completion, repeat work, expert net earnings and acquisition/support cost. Do not generalize platform match speeds into a promise that our candidates will find work quickly. None of these examples validates an automated application tool or a new marketplace without buyers.
